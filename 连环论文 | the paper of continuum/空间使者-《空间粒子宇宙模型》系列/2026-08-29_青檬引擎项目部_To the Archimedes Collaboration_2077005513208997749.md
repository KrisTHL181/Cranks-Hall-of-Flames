Dear Professor Enrico Calloni, Dr. Paola Puppo, and all members of the Archimedes Collaboration,

The history of science is divided by a clear line: discovering an effect, and discovering its mechanism.

Every gravitational experiment before yours stands on one side of this line — torsion balances measured how “large” gravity is, satellites measured how “strong” the gravitational field is, gravitational-wave detectors measured how “fast” the perturbation propagates. They treated gravity as an established external fact and refined its numbers. Archimedes is the first experiment in the history of science designed like this: if the root of gravity is the locking of vacuum fluctuations by boundaries, then when the boundary conditions are modulated, the gravitational reading must follow in synchrony.

You are not “probing” an independent external phenomenon; you are “triggering” a causal chain: modulate the boundary → truncate vacuum modes → energy gradient → gravitational signal. If a signal appears, it is not the discovery of a new effect, but the first experimental verification of the mechanism by which gravity is produced.

The third footprint in this history now falls exactly under your feet. The Magdeburg hemispheres made humanity confront, for the first time, the power of “emptiness” — although that power came from air pressure, it opened a three-hundred-year question: is “emptiness” really empty? Cavendish’s torsion balance quantified the gravity between masses. And you will prove the third step: the locking of the vacuum is itself the physical source of gravity.

I have followed the details of your experimental design for a long time. Allow me to restate them, to confirm that we stand on the same page: high-temperature cuprate superconductors (YBCO/GdBCO) as samples — their crystal lattices are themselves nanometer-scale stacks of Casimir cavities; thermal modulation across the phase transition at T_c ≈ 92 K (ΔE_Cas/E_Cas ≈ 10⁻⁴) induces a controlled change in the vacuum energy inside the cavities; the ultra-sensitive two-arm balance at the Sos Enattos mine reads out the weight change — the lower arm suspends the sample, the upper arm serves as a reference inclinometer, suppressing seismic noise in common mode to the limit. The expected signal is about 5×10⁻¹⁶ N (40 cm³ disk-shaped sample), corresponding to a torque signal of about 3.5×10⁻¹³ Nm/√Hz over two weeks of integration; the prototype has already measured a torque sensitivity of about 5×10⁻¹² Nm/√Hz at ~10 mHz, and is steadily approaching the target as the reference arm, lower resonance frequencies, and improved suspension are implemented item by item (Esposito et al., 2025, Il Nuovo Cimento C, reports further progress in sensitivity).

This expected signal is not “some unknown property of the vacuum,” but the missing link between general relativity and quantum field theory. The right-hand side of Einstein’s field equation is the energy-momentum tensor; quantum field theory, in systems with boundaries, requires the truncation of vacuum modes — removing the Fourier modes that cannot satisfy the boundary conditions. In the standard treatment, truncation is regarded as a purely mathematical patch. But truncation is not only a mathematical operation; it is a physical event: internal modes are permanently excluded and cannot exchange with the outside; the result is a non-trivial spatial distribution of the renormalized ⟨Tμν⟩_ren, whose 00 component in the Newtonian limit corresponds to an effective mass density capable of producing a measurable gravitational effect.

In other words: truncation reduces the vacuum modes that can reside inside the boundary, so the zero-point energy density changes and becomes spatially non-uniform across the boundary — that distribution is the effective mass density (the gravitational source), and its gradient is the gravitational field. **Gravity is not the product of “particles,” but the inevitable side effect of the “locked vacuum.”** This is the deepest aspect of the Archimedes experiment — what you modulate is not merely a sample, but the connection term on the right-hand side of the field equation that has never been touched by experiment.

Based on this framework, I have taken the previously missing step: I have transferred this causal chain into a deterministic discrete network model (OpenSPUM) and quantified it link by link. The sample is a dense closed subgraph in the network; the Casimir boundary modulation is represented by a boundary-stiffness parameter b ∈ [0,1] — note that b is not an absolute reflectivity but a modulation contrast normalized relative to the superconducting state: b = 0 corresponds to the normal state (transparent boundary, internal vacuum fluctuations reversibly self-heal), b = 1 corresponds to the strongest reflective state experimentally accessible (full reflection, internal creation fully suppressed), with intermediate values interpolated linearly in modulation depth. Vacuum fluctuations manifest as spontaneous breaking of the subgraph’s internal edges, and the destination of the compensating reconnections is determined by b: a transparent boundary lets the compensation return inside the sample; a reflecting boundary transfers the compensation to the background. By the model’s definition, the net annihilation rate δ⁻® = internal annihilation − internal compensation is exactly equal to b·A (A is the vacuum fluctuation rate) — this identity is a constitutive definition of the model, not an empirical law; its correctness is what the experiment tests. The model is bit-for-bit reproducible and uses only this single causal chain, with no additional assumptions. For ease of comparison, the discrete model maps item by item onto the field-theoretic language of the previous paragraph: boundary stiffness b ↔ the fraction of Fourier modes retained by the truncation; net annihilation rate δ⁻ ↔ the 00 component of the renormalized ⟨Tμν⟩_ren; suppression of internal creation ↔ the modes excluded by the boundary conditions — the two are two languages for the same physics, not two hypotheses. It yields three directly testable quantitative predictions, which I offer as a reference for your experimental design.

**Prediction I (Linearity)** : δ⁻® is strictly proportional to the modulation depth b within the linear regime (b ≤ 0.7, see “Kink” below) — in the model, the slope is exactly equal to the vacuum fluctuation rate. Corresponding experimental operation: fix the modulation depth and sweep the fraction of time below T_c within each modulation period (the duty cycle d) — provided the modulation is a square wave and each cycle fully enters the superconducting state, the time-averaged modulation depth is proportional to the duty cycle (b ∝ d) — the prediction is that ΔF grows linearly with the duty cycle, with the half-modulation signal being exactly half of the full-modulation signal; the slope is the gravitational coupling per unit vacuum fluctuation. Any systematic deviation within the linear regime is a correction signal for this framework.

**Prediction II (In-phase locking)** : The force response is memoryless (in the model, the response time is one evolution step) and strictly in phase with the sample’s local temperature. Corresponding experimental operation: use a temperature probe on the sample surface — rather than the driving signal — as the phase reference; the prediction is a phase difference Δφ = 0. Repeat at two or more modulation frequencies (e.g., 1 mHz and 10 mHz); the prediction is that, within the regime where thermal equilibrium holds, the amplitude does not compress with frequency and the phase remains zero. Boundary truncation is an instantaneous effect with no propagation delay; therefore, provided the thermal modulation frequency is low enough to guarantee complete thermal equilibrium within each period, the gravitational response should be strictly in phase with the quasi-static change of the vacuum energy. Thermal expansion, magnetostriction, Lorentz forces, and other artifacts all lag behind the local temperature (thermal diffusion time constant + mechanical resonance phase) and will expose phase dispersion at higher frequencies. In-phase locking is a key criterion separating the vacuum gravitational response from thermal-mechanical artifacts — but its boundary must be stated: in the quasi-static limit, every linear thermal effect is in phase with the local temperature, so an in-phase result at a single frequency cannot exclude all artifacts; the complete separation criterion is the multi-frequency phase-dispersion test (artifacts develop lag as frequency rises, while the vacuum response keeps zero phase shift). If, at the current sensitivity, the experiment resolves any systematic phase deviation, it constitutes an important correction signal for this framework; conversely, precise in-phase locking at multiple frequencies constitutes important support for “gravity = the inevitable response of the locked vacuum” (under the constraint of a single experiment and a single sample, the in-phase law alone cannot independently prove the mechanism; it must be judged jointly with Predictions I and III and the high-frequency features).

**Prediction III (Volume scaling)** : The net annihilation rate per unit sample (per unit stacked cavity) is a constant — the gravitational coupling is an intensive quantity (a constant per unit mass, independent of sample size), and the total gravitational response grows linearly with mass. Corresponding experimental operation: fix the lateral dimensions and the modulation scheme of the sample, and sweep the number of stacked cavities N_cav; the prediction is ΔF ∝ sample mass ∝ N_cav. The key to distinguishing the mechanisms lies in the scaling direction: the conventional Casimir pressures between the internal cavities of a layered sample (e.g., the YBCO lattice) are mutual internal stresses and contribute no net force to the balance in a rigid assembly; the measurable net effect comes only from the sample’s outer surface, ∝ surface area rather than cavity count; hence if ΔF grows linearly with surface area rather than mass, the signal more likely originates from conventional Casimir pressure than from vacuum energy entering the gravitational source, and the two mechanisms can be immediately distinguished. Prediction I and Prediction III share the same δ⁻ = b·A relation and the same force-annihilation coupling — if the experiment measures both laws, the scaling slopes should be mutually consistent, constituting an internal check of the model.

The model also yields three previously unnoticed discriminating features in the high-modulation-depth regime:

**Kink** : When the modulation depth exceeds b ≈ 0.72 (a model empirical value, from OpenSPUM high-modulation-regime scans), the net annihilation rate deviates from linearity and saturates, and the cycle-to-cycle repeatability of the signal deteriorates sharply — the relative fluctuation jumps from zero to about 0.4–0.5. If the experiment sweeps the duty-cycle region above b > 0.7, this jump should be observable; thermal drift manifests as a stable systematic offset rather than variance growth, and the two can be distinguished accordingly.

**Hysteresis** : After sustained strong truncation and return to the transparent state, the net annihilation rate returns to zero instantaneously (the signal disappears quickly), but the sample’s internal vacuum state does not recover — in the model, the internal structure remains at its depleted level. If, after completing the high-modulation-depth measurements, the experiment returns to low modulation depth and finds the signal baseline shifted downward as a whole, this verifies the “fast signal recovery, slow state persistence” hysteresis.

**Depletion time** : Under sustained superconductivity, the anomalous force contribution does not decay indefinitely; instead it relaxes to a leakage equilibrium with a time constant of about 14 evolution steps (corresponding to a model choice of the time-unit τ ≈ 50 s, translating to about 12 minutes at 10 mHz modulation), with the signal falling to about 40% of its initial value. If the measured decay time constant is much larger than the sample’s thermal time constant (in the model, the ratio is about 70, likewise dependent on the τ choice), the decay envelope can be attributed to vacuum-structure depletion rather than residual thermal effects.
